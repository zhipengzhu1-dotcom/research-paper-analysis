3.1 Asynchronous reinforcement learning
The objective in Equation 5 cannot be optimized by directly backpropagating through the discrete sampling of
rollouts. Instead, policy-gradient methods such as REINFORCE (Williams, 1992; Sutton et al., 1999), PPO (Schulman
et al., 2017), and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) are widely used to optimize Equation 5 for autoregressive language mod-
els. Strictly on-policy training requires optimization to wait for a new batch of rollouts from the current policy,
which is inefficient when rollouts involve multiple turns and expensive tool calls. We therefore carry out training
asynchronously using prime-rl (Prime Intellect, 2025). During optimization, separate inference and trainer workers
operate concurrently; the inference workers continuously generate rollouts, while the trainer workers update the
underlying policy. As optimization proceeds, updated policy weights are broadcast to the inference workers while
rollouts remain in flight, so later actions in a multi-turn rollout may be generated by a newer policy version than
earlier actions. This improves training throughput but introduces trainer–inference mismatch, which we account
for using the IPO objective described below.
