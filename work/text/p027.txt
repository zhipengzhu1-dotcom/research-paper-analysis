C.5 Chemical analysis of designs
Lastly, we analyze both how the chemistry of the seed molecule influences model behavior and the chemical design
principles that the models leverage when carrying out optimization. In Figure A8, we look at the performance of the
trained and frontier models on anionic and neutral seed molecules. When the seed molecule carries an anionic
group, curriculum-trained models outperform other models by the largest margin. For neutrally charged seed
molecules, the relative differences are much more muted, with the curriculum-trained models performing similarly
to frontier models.
    Across all anionic modifications in all model rollouts, on average, removing an anionic group improved the
relative binding affinity by 26.1 kcal/mol, with larger values indicating stronger predicted binding. In Figure A9, we
plot the percentage of scored candidates that remove an anionic group and find that the curriculum-trained models
overwhelmingly favor removing these groups from candidates.
