    Inspired by recent work which has shown that curriculum-style reinforcement learning is beneficial for learning
across a mixture of tasks with varying difficulty (Parashar et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026), we also explore how various
training curricula impact downstream performance. We define various classes of curricula that we use for training
in Table 1 and discuss results in Section 6. We consider both the effect of training directly on Tier-1 tasks and Tier-2
tasks starting from the Qwen 3.6 (base) model for 500 steps (denoted by T1(500) and T2(500) in Table 1, respectively)
and mixing the two tasks in different stages. T1(a)–T2(b) denotes training for a steps on Tier-1 tasks followed by b
steps on only Tier-2 tasks and T1(a)–Mix(b) denotes training for a steps on Tier-1 tasks followed by b steps on a
25% : 75% mixture of Tier-1 and Tier-2 tasks. We find that training on Tier-1 tasks alone transfers to the downstream
SQM evaluation, closing much of the gap between Qwen 3.6 (base) and frontier models. Additionally, we observe
that adding the Tier-2 tasks in both the T1(a)–T2(b) and T1(a)–Mix(b) cases improves performance over Tier-1
only training, with trained models exceeding frontier-level performance. While we do not obtain a clear “scaling
law” where increased cost leads to increased performance, we do see that the more expensive curriculum training
approaches outperform the cheaper Tier-1 only training.
