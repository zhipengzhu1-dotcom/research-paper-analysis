7 Conclusion
In this work, we investigate improving the design capabilities of LLMs for high-fidelity and high-cost chemical
properties. Specifically, we design a large number of synthetic design tasks and explore how various training recipes
that leverage these synthetic tasks can improve performance. We find that the best performing recipes enable the
model to efficiently design molecules and demonstrate capabilities that exceed larger and far more capable frontier
models. While we only focused on in silico properties in this work, the strategies we employed can also be used
in experimental contexts, where training on high-throughput and low-fidelity experimental tasks can potentially
generalize to low-throughput and high-fidelity experimental tasks. The training recipes here ultimately serve as a
foundation for enabling better small-molecule design capabilities in LLMs. Deploying LLMs to run full drug design
campaigns will no doubt require richer training recipes and the incorporation of other training approaches (e.g.,
supervised finetuning, on-policy distillation) that can ultimately teach the model how to make more robust and
sensible chemical designs. We leave exploration of those topics to future work.
